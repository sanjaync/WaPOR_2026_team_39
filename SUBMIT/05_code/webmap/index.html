Same Budget, More Water

IPAT — the irrigation assessment tool already running at Egypt's Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation — shows where water productivity is high and low across 1.5 million ha of the Middle and West Delta. It stops there. A ministry with a fixed budget still has to decide where to spend.

This is the layer that answers that. WaPOR v3 separates transpiration — water that grew a crop — from evaporation, water that left the field having grown nothing. We turn that split into a ranked spending list: which areas get the on-farm modernisation budget, in what order, and how much water each dollar buys back.

    Assumptions — change them and watch the answer move

    These are literature-order estimates, not measurements. The ranking depends on them, so they are exposed rather than buried. If you have better figures, type them in — everything above recalculates.

    Method

    Recoverable water = Emm × irrigated ha × 10 × reduction fraction.
    Annual cost = (USD/ha ÷ life years) × ha. Ranked by USD per m³.
    Budget spent cheapest-water-first. The baseline splits the same budget by irrigated area — a neutral prior, not a documented description of ministry practice. One measure per governorate. Water saved is annualised, so measures lasting through the winter season are credited for both.

    Data

    Upstream tool: IPAT, the irrigation assessment tool hosted by Egypt's MWRI (WaPOR v3 Level 3, 20 m, Middle & West Delta, ~1.5 Mha).
    This layer: FAO WaPOR v3 Level 2 (100 m, dekadal) AETI, E, T, I.
    Cropland mask: seasonal AETI > 200 mm — in Egypt, ET is its own cropland mask.
    Boundaries: Natural Earth admin-1 (public domain).
    Protocol after WaPORIPA.

    Known weaknesses

    Intervention costs and reduction fractions are estimates.
    Cultivated-area figures are approximate planning numbers, not CAPMAS/MALR statistics.
    Evaporation saved on-field is not automatically water saved at basin scale — some returns to the aquifer or a drain and is reused downstream.
    Suppressing soil evaporation can convert E to transpiration and yield rather than to saved water, so recoverable E is an upper bound on volume.
    ETLook's E/T partitioning is least constrained over standing water — which is exactly where our signal is.
    Administrative polygons contain large desert areas; hatched governorates are mostly desert and their statistics describe only the irrigated fraction inside them.
