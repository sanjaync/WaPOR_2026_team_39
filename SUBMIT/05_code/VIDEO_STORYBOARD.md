# 1-minute pitch video — shot-by-shot

**Team 39 · "Same Budget, More Water" · Phase 1 submission**

Total 60 s. Screen recording of `webmap/index.html` with voiceover. No talking head
needed; if you want one, use it only for shots 1 and 7.

**Before you record**

- Set the browser to full screen, hide bookmarks, zoom to 100%.
- Reset the app: budget slider to **$12M**, equity floor to **0**, map metric to
  **Evaporation (mm)**, light theme.
- Record at 1920×1080. QuickTime, OBS or Loom all work.
- **The red DEMO banner must be visible in every frame that shows a number.** Do not
  scroll it off, do not crop it out. If a juror clones the repo and finds synthetic
  data behind a confident number, nothing else in the pitch survives.

## Direction notes

**Shot 4 is the pitch.** It is the only place the jury sees a decision being made
rather than described. Drag the slider slowly enough that the list visibly re-orders
— about two seconds of travel. Do not talk over the re-order; let it land.

**Shot 5 is the differentiator.** Every previous winner in this hackathon predicts,
recommends or forecasts. None of them audits its own information value in public.
Land on the **0%** bar and pause for half a beat before speaking again.

**Shot 7 is what they will remember.** It answers the objection they were forming
during shot 4 — *"you can't verify any of this"* — before they get to ask it. End on
the word "worked" and cut. No thank-you slide, no logo animation; you do not have the
seconds.

**Pace.** 155 words in 60 seconds is a relaxed newsreader pace. Do not rush to fit
more in. If you are running long, cut shot 2 to five seconds — the jury already knows
what IPAT is.

## What to leave out, and why

Each of these invites a question you cannot answer in the time it buys you.

- **"Fifty percent more water."** Against a literal equal split the number looks far
  bigger than against an area-proportional one. Quoting the big number against the weak
  comparator is the fastest way to lose this jury. Say ~20% and name the comparator.
- **Naming Kafr El Sheikh, Damietta and Beheira as the answer.** All three border the
  northern lakes, and until real masked rasters are loaded the ranking is provisional.
  Show the *mechanism* — dark district versus light district — not the verdict.
- **"At 100 metres, every ten days."** Invites *"IPAT already has 20 m, why did you drop
  to 100?"* and *"your decision is annual, why do you need dekadal?"* The resolution is
  not the argument.
- **"Almost nobody uses the E/T split."** You are saying it to the people who built
  WaPOR. The true and stronger version is *"nobody converts it into money."*
- **The four-intervention table.** Two of the four are never selected at any budget.
  Naming alternate wetting and drying hands a juror a free objection about whether it
  reduces evaporation or percolation. If you name a measure, name **laser land
  levelling** — already deployed at scale in Egypt, and what the model actually picks.
- **SDG numbers.** Box-ticking. Zero persuasive force with this jury, and 6.4.1 is
  value-added per water withdrawn, which you do not compute.
- **Every feature.** The slider, the editable assumptions, the offline single-file
  build, the one-click brief export. None of it is a decision. It belongs on the Miro
  board, where jurors read at their own pace.

## If you have to cut to 45 seconds

Keep shots 1, 4, 6, 7. Drop 2, 3 and 5, and fold the insight into shot 4:
*"WaPOR separates water that grew a crop from water that evaporated. We rank every
governorate by how much of the second is recoverable, and what it costs to get back."*

You lose the self-audit, which is a real loss — but the decision and the check survive,
and those are the two things nobody else will have.
