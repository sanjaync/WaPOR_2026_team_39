# The 1-minute pitch — script, shots, and how it scores

**"Same Budget, More Water" · WaPOR Hackathon 2026**

The two things submitted are **the 1-minute video** and **the Miro board**. This
file is the video. Everything technical belongs on the board, not in the sixty
seconds.

---

## The rules, and what they cost you if you miss them

**Must not appear anywhere in the pitch:**

- team member names or countries
- your team number
- **the theme you selected** — do not say "Governance" or name a subtheme
- **next steps** — no roadmap, no "in Phase 2 we will…", no future work
- heavy technical detail — no dekads, no resolutions, no formulas, no band names

That third and fourth one are easy to trip over, because they feel like good
practice everywhere else. Cut them.

**How the 100 points are allocated** — write the script to this, not to what is
most interesting to you:

| Weight | Criterion | Where it lands in the script |
|:---:|---|---|
| **25%** | Alignment of the WaPOR tool with the challenge *and* the mandate | shots 2–3 |
| **20%** | Decision-makers and their mandate, clearly defined | shot 1 |
| **20%** | Innovation and realistic approach turning tools into decisions and impact | shots 4–5 |
| **15%** | Clearly defined challenge | shot 2 |
| **10%** | Feasibility of embedding the tool in decision making | shots 3 and 6 |
| **5%** | Pitch quality — clear, engaging message | throughout |
| **5%** | Miro board completeness and effort | not in the video |

**45% of the score is the mandate and its alignment with the tool.** Not the
map, not the method, not the finding. Name the institution, name what it is
legally responsible for, name the decision it makes, and show the tool sitting
inside that responsibility. Most teams will spend their sixty seconds on the
technology.

---

## Format: screen capture

Of the four formats the organisers describe — simple talking head, multiscene,
screen capture, animated — **screen capture is the obvious one for you**, because
you have something almost nobody else will: a working tool where a decision
visibly gets made. Record the screen, narrate over it. No animation software
needed, and nothing to learn tonight.

Record the screen and the voice **separately** if you can. Re-doing one narration
take is far easier than re-doing the whole capture, and you will re-do the
narration three or four times.

---

## The hook

The organisers' own advice lists "a contradiction of conventional wisdom" as a way
to open. You have a real one, and it was buried at shot 5 in the previous
version of this file:

> **We thought Egypt's rice paddies wasted the most water. They waste the least.**

That is true, it is counter-intuitive, it came out of your own measurement, and it
takes nine seconds. It now opens the pitch. Everything else follows from it, and
the jury is listening by second three instead of second twenty.

---

## The script — 144 words, about 58 seconds

> **[hook · 9s]** We thought Egypt's rice paddies wasted the most water. They
> waste the least. And that one measurement changes where a national budget
> should go.
>
> **[mandate · 13s]** Egypt's Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation makes a
> fixed share of the Nile feed a hundred and ten million people. Every year it
> spends a budget modernising farms — and has to justify it.
>
> **[challenge · 7s]** But nothing tells it *where*. So the money spreads by
> cultivated area, or by political balance.
>
> **[alignment · 10s]** The ministry already runs a WaPOR tool that shows where
> water productivity is low. It doesn't say where to spend. We add that step.
>
> **[decision · 14s]** WaPOR separates water that grew a crop from water that
> simply evaporated. We rank every governorate by how much of that is recoverable
> and what it costs — an ordered spending list. Same budget, a quarter more water.
>
> **[close · 5s]** And next season, the same satellite shows whether the money
> worked.

**Open `teleprompter.html`** on a second screen or a phone. It scrolls this script
at the right pace, shows what should be on screen for each line, and counts you
in. Space to start, R to reset.

## Direction

**Shot 1 buys you the room.** Pause a full beat after "They waste the least."
The sentence only works if it is allowed to land.

**Shots 2–4 carry 45% of the score. Do not rush them.** Thirty seconds on the
institution and the tool's fit inside it will feel slow to you and will read as
rigour to the jury. Say "Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation" in full,
once.

**Shot 5 is the only place a decision visibly gets made.** Drag the slider over
about two seconds and let the list re-order without narrating it. Silence over
motion is fine; it draws the eye.

**Shot 6 ends it.** Cut on "worked". No thank-you slide, no logo, no music sting.

**Pace.** 144 words in 58 seconds is a relaxed newsreader. Do not chase the timer
— landing at 1:02 is fine, and rushing the mandate to save four seconds costs you
20% of the score.

**Practise it five times before you record once.** The organisers put "practice,
practice, practice" in their guidance for a reason: a pitch this short is judged
partly on whether the ideas connect, and that only comes from repetition.

## Know your room

The jury is unusually strong on exactly the weaknesses this concept has. Read
these before you record — two of them change what you say.

- **A water-accounting specialist (IWMI, MENA irrigation).** Will know
  immediately that evaporation saved on a field is not water saved in a closed
  basin. **This is why the script says "recoverable", never "saved".** Keep that
  word discipline. If asked, concede it in one sentence and say the figures rank
  places rather than close a water account.
- **A social scientist working on the politics of knowledge and the
  technomediation of climate.** Will be alert to a tool that quietly
  depoliticises an allocation decision that is properly political. Your answer
  is the equity floor: it does not hide the politics, it prices them and hands
  the dial to the committee. That framing is worth having ready — it turns the
  sharpest likely critique into your strongest feature.
- **A government decision-support practitioner** who has built exactly this kind
  of system inside a state water department. Will care about institutional
  embedding above all. Emphasise that this runs on an existing tool, in an
  existing meeting, on an existing budget cycle.
- **Two remote-sensing applications specialists**, including one at the World
  Bank focused on turning EO into decisions. They are the audience for "ordered
  spending list, not another map" — and they will spot an overstated accuracy
  claim instantly. Do not inflate anything.

## What to leave out, and why

- **"Governance", or any subtheme name.** Explicitly prohibited.
- **Any roadmap or future work.** Explicitly prohibited, and it costs you nothing
  to drop — the concept stands without it.
- **Dekads, 100-metre resolution, band names, the three formulas.** Prohibited as
  excess technical detail, and none of it earns a point. It belongs on the Miro
  board.
- **"A quarter more water" beyond one mention.** The comparator matters and you
  have no time to explain it. Say it once, move on.
- **Naming a winning governorate.** Aswan led partly on water-body contamination
  until we caught it, and the honest ranking is a gradient rather than a verdict.
  Show the mechanism — dark against light — not the answer.
- **The self-audit numbers.** Real per-district evaporation gives +25.2%, a
  per-system average +21.5%, spatially flat 0%. Excellent for a question, far too
  technical for the pitch.
- **Every feature you built.** The sliders, the editable assumptions, the offline
  build, the brief export. None of them is a decision.

---

## If you have to cut to 45 seconds

Keep shots 1, 3, 4, 6. Drop 2 and 5, and fold the challenge into shot 1:
*"…and nothing tells it where to spend."* You lose the finding, which hurts, but
the mandate and the decision survive — and those carry 45 points to the
finding's 20.
